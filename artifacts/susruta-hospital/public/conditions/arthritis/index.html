Arthritis in Ayurveda

Ayurveda describes two main types of arthritis based on their pathology:

Amavata (corresponding to Rheumatoid Arthritis) — caused by impaired digestive fire (Agni) producing undigested toxic material (Ama) that combines with Vata dosha and lodges in the joints. Characterised by symmetrical joint inflammation, morning stiffness, and systemic symptoms.

Sandhivata (corresponding to Osteoarthritis) — caused by depletion of Vata and the natural joint fluid (Shleshaka Kapha), leading to cartilage wear, joint crepitus, and pain that worsens with movement and cold weather.

Understanding which type is dominant — and what combination of doshas is involved — is the first step in Dr. P. Murali Krishna's treatment approach.

Treatment Approach at Susruta Hospital

  1. Initial AssessmentDetailed Nadi Pariksha (pulse diagnosis) and Prakriti analysis to determine the type of arthritis, doshic involvement, and digestive fire status. Assessment of all current medications and disease duration.
  2. Ama Pachana (Toxin Digestion)For Amavata, the first stage is digesting accumulated Ama using light diet, fasting protocols, and deepana-pachana herbs before initiating Panchakarma. This is a critical step often missed in non-Ayurvedic settings.
  3. Panchakarma DetoxificationVirechana (purgation) to eliminate Pitta and Ama; Basti (medicated enema) — the most important treatment to correct the root Vata imbalance. A course of 16–30 Basti procedures typically produces significant improvement.
  4. Localised TreatmentsKati Basti (for lumbar involvement), Janu Basti (for knee joints), Patra Pinda Sweda (herbal leaf bolus fomentation), and Abhyanga to reduce localised pain and inflammation.
  5. Herbal MedicinesPersonalised herbal formulations prescribed based on the individual's condition — typically including Guggulu preparations (Yogaraj Guggulu, Mahayogaraj Guggulu), Rasna, Punarnava, Shunthi, and anti-inflammatory Rasa preparations.
  6. Dietary and Lifestyle GuidanceArthritis management requires significant dietary changes. Specific foods to avoid and include are prescribed. Daily routine recommendations to support Vata balance and prevent Ama accumulation.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Can Ayurveda cure arthritis?
    Ayurveda can significantly reduce arthritis pain, stiffness, and inflammation, and in many cases achieve long-term remission — particularly for Rheumatoid Arthritis and Osteoarthritis in early to moderate stages. The treatment addresses the root cause — Vata imbalance and Ama — rather than just suppressing symptoms.
  • Can I continue my arthritis medications during Ayurvedic treatment?
    Yes. Ayurvedic treatment can be initiated alongside existing allopathic medications. As the condition improves under Ayurvedic care, allopathic medication doses are typically reduced in consultation with your rheumatologist. Do not discontinue any medication without medical supervision.
  • How long does Ayurvedic treatment for arthritis take to show results?
    Most patients experience noticeable improvement in pain and mobility within 2–4 weeks. A complete Panchakarma course (14–28 days) produces significant results. Long-term Ayurvedic medicines are continued for 3–6 months to consolidate improvement.
  • Is there a special diet to follow for arthritis?
    Yes. For Amavata (Rheumatoid Arthritis), avoid dairy, non-vegetarian food, cold/heavy foods, and incompatible food combinations. For Sandhivata (Osteoarthritis), a Vata-pacifying diet with warm, moist, easily digestible foods is recommended. Dr. P. Murali Krishna provides detailed dietary guidelines specific to your constitution and condition.
