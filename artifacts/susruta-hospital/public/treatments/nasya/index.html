What is Nasya?

Nasya (Sanskrit: नस्य) is one of the five Panchakarma procedures. According to Ayurveda, the nose is the Shirasodwara — the gateway to the head, brain, and consciousness. Medicated oils, herbal powders, or fresh herbal juices administered through the nasal passage directly reach and cleanse the sinuses, nasal cavity, throat, trachea, and brain channels (Shrotas).

Nasya is the primary treatment for all conditions above the shoulders — including the eyes, ears, nose, throat, neck, and the central nervous system. It eliminates accumulated Kapha dosha from the upper respiratory tract and head, and clears congestion in the brain channels.

Conditions Treated by Nasya

Sinusitis & Allergic Rhinitis

Nasya is the most effective Ayurvedic treatment for chronic sinusitis, allergic rhinitis, and nasal polyps — eliminating Kapha and reducing inflammation in the sinuses.

Migraine & Chronic Headaches

Nasya with appropriate medicated oil reduces the Vata-Pitta imbalance in the head that causes migraines, cluster headaches, and chronic tension headaches.

Facial Paralysis (Bell's Palsy)

A key treatment for Bell's palsy and other conditions affecting the facial nerves — Nasya nourishes the nerve channels supplying the face and head.

Cervical Spondylosis

Nasya, in combination with Basti, is a highly effective treatment for cervical spondylosis and neck stiffness by addressing Vata imbalance in the upper spine and head.

Hair & Eye Conditions

Premature greying, hair loss, dry eyes, and eye strain respond well to Nasya — which nourishes the channels supplying the scalp and eyes.

Neurological Conditions

Parkinson's disease, epilepsy, and other neurological conditions benefit from Nasya as a supportive therapy to nourish and cleanse the brain channels.

How Nasya is Performed

  1. Preparatory MassageGentle massage of the face, forehead, neck, and upper back with warm medicated oil to relax the tissues and open the channels.
  2. Swedana (Steam)Gentle steam applied to the face and nasal area to further soften and open the channels for better absorption of the medication.
  3. Nasya AdministrationThe patient lies with their head slightly tilted back. 2–8 drops of the prescribed medicated substance are instilled into each nostril. The patient inhales gently. This is repeated for 3–7 days as part of a course.
  4. GarglingThe patient gently spits out any medicine that flows to the throat and gargles with warm water.
  5. RestThe patient rests for 15–20 minutes after the procedure, keeping warm and avoiding cold air.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Is Nasya uncomfortable?
    Most patients find Nasya mildly unfamiliar initially but not painful. A slight burning or tingling sensation is normal and passes quickly. The preparatory massage makes the experience much more comfortable.
  • How many sessions of Nasya are needed?
    A standard course is 7 consecutive days. For chronic conditions like sinusitis or spondylosis, longer courses or repeated cycles may be needed. Dr. P. Murali Krishna will prescribe the appropriate duration.
  • Can Nasya be done at home?
    Pratimarsha Nasya (daily preventive — 2 drops of sesame or Anu Taila) can be performed at home safely. Therapeutic Nasya for conditions must be performed by a qualified practitioner at the clinic.
  • What time of day is best for Nasya?
    Morning after sunrise, on an empty stomach or at least 1 hour after eating. Avoid Nasya immediately after a heavy meal, during fever, or in very cold weather.
