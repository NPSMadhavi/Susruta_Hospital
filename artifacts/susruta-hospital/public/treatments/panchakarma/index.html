What is Panchakarma?

Panchakarma (Sanskrit: पञ्चकर्म) literally means "five actions." It is the cornerstone of Ayurvedic medicine — a systematic, physician-supervised programme designed to eliminate deep-seated toxins (Ama) from the body's tissues, restore balance among the three doshas (Vata, Pitta, Kapha), and rejuvenate the entire system.

Unlike general wellness massages or detox diets, authentic Panchakarma is a complete medical process prescribed only after a thorough Ayurvedic assessment of the patient's Prakriti (constitution) and Vikriti (current imbalances). At Susruta Hospital, each programme is customised to the individual patient.

The Five Procedures of Panchakarma

1. Vamana (Therapeutic Emesis)

Medicated emesis is used to eliminate excess Kapha dosha from the stomach and upper respiratory tract. It is particularly effective for bronchial asthma, chronic cough, skin disorders, and obesity. The patient undergoes preparatory oleation and sudation before the procedure.

2. Virechana (Therapeutic Purgation)

Controlled purgation using herbal laxatives to eliminate excess Pitta dosha from the small intestine and liver. Highly effective for skin diseases, liver disorders, digestive problems, inflammatory conditions, and hormonal imbalances.

3. Basti (Medicated Enema)

Considered the most powerful Panchakarma procedure for Vata disorders. Medicated oils (Anuvasana Basti) or herbal decoctions (Niruha Basti) are administered rectally to cleanse the colon. Highly effective for arthritis, constipation, neurological conditions, and lower back pain. Learn more about Basti therapy.

4. Nasya (Nasal Therapy)

Medicated oils, powders, or herbal juices administered through the nasal passage — the "gateway to the head." Eliminates Kapha accumulated in the sinuses, throat, nose, and head. Effective for sinusitis, migraine, facial paralysis, and neurological conditions. Learn more about Nasya therapy.

5. Raktamokshana (Bloodletting Therapy)

Selective removal of vitiated blood using techniques such as leech therapy (Jalaukavacharana) or venesection. Used for skin diseases, inflammatory conditions, gout, and localised blood disorders where blood purification is needed.

The Three Stages of Panchakarma

  1. Purva Karma — Preparatory Phase (3–7 days) The body is prepared for purification through Snehana (internal and external oleation with medicated ghee/oils) and Swedana (herbal steam therapy). This loosens and mobilises toxins from the tissues into the digestive tract for elimination.
  2. Pradhana Karma — Main Procedures The selected Panchakarma procedure(s) are administered based on the patient's condition and the physician's prescription. Not all five procedures are performed in every patient — Dr. P. Murali Krishna selects the most appropriate combination.
  3. Paschat Karma — Post-treatment Care (1–4 weeks) A carefully supervised recovery phase with specific dietary guidelines (Samsarjana Krama), gradual return to normal activity, and Rasayana (rejuvenating) therapies to consolidate the benefits of Panchakarma.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • How many days does Panchakarma take?
    A complete Panchakarma programme spans 14–28 days including the preparatory phase, main procedures, and post-treatment care. The exact duration depends on your condition and constitution. Short 7-day programmes targeting specific conditions are also available.
  • Is Panchakarma painful?
    Panchakarma procedures are generally well-tolerated. The preparatory oleation and massage (Abhyanga) are deeply relaxing. Some procedures like Vamana may cause temporary discomfort but are conducted with care and medication to minimise any side effects. The physician monitors you throughout.
  • Can I continue my regular medications during Panchakarma?
    Please inform Dr. P. Murali Krishna about all medications you are currently taking during your initial consultation. He will advise on how to manage existing medications alongside the Panchakarma programme. Do not discontinue any prescribed medications without your doctor's guidance.
  • Is Panchakarma suitable for all ages?
    Panchakarma can be adapted for most age groups, from young adults to the elderly. However, it is generally not recommended for children under 12, pregnant women, or those with severe debilitating conditions. A thorough assessment will determine suitability.
